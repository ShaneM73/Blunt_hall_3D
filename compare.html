Which method draws the walls best?

The 3rd floor of Roy Blunt Hall (19 million cleaned LiDAR points, about 37 m × 137 m) was turned into a wall model three ways: our own geometric block model, Cloud2BIM (point cloud → IFC) and Meta's SceneScript (point cloud → a learned "scene language"). Every result was scored the same way against the measured walls. Then the same three methods were run again on a 3½-minute walkthrough video, rebuilt in 3D by LingBot-Map without any LiDAR (section 05).

03 · Score itCompleteness, precision and doors

Completeness: share of the measured wall points within 10 cm of a modelled wall. Precision: share of the modelled wall centre line within 10 cm (+ half the local wall thickness) of a measured wall point. F: their harmonic mean. Scored on the plan at 2.5 cm so thick walls (blocks, IFC solids) and thin planes (SceneScript) count alike.

04 · Tune itSceneScript needs room-sized pieces

SceneScript reads at most a 32 m cube (640 bins of 5 cm) and was trained on single rooms seen through Aria glasses. The floor was cut into overlapping square tiles; each wall or door is kept by the tile whose centre holds it. Each dot is one full run over the floor; hover for details.

05 · From a videoNo LiDAR: the same methods on a walkthrough video

On 10 Nov 2026 the 3rd-floor aisle was filmed with a hand-held DJI camera (1080p, 3 min 35 s), from the stairwell around the light-well block. LingBot-Map, a feed-forward streaming 3D reconstruction model, turned the frames into a depth map and a camera pose each. The depth maps were filtered and fused into a mesh, then scaled and aligned to the LiDAR (the video has no real size or direction). The video mesh was then given to the same three wall methods as the LiDAR. The LiDAR is used only to align and score the result.

Wall models from the video vs from the LiDAR, in the walked area

Scored like section 03, but only within 3 m of the camera path (the part of the floor the video could see), so the LiDAR and video models are judged on the same walls. At 10 cm tolerance the score mostly measures the video's alignment error (median 20 cm); at 25 cm it shows whether the wall layout itself is right. Block method is our block rules applied straight to points; on the LiDAR it is a control and an upper bound, since it is built from the same points it is scored against.

06 · What we foundFindings

0.88

Scan geometry wins

Our block model fits wall faces directly in the cleaned LiDAR (15 cm blocks on the building axes), so it follows the real walls best: 84 % of the measured walls covered, 92 % of its walls on a real wall.

0.26 → 0.74

SceneScript is a room-scale model

Fed 28 m tiles it misses most walls; at 8–10 m tiles (about one room) it reaches F 0.72–0.74. Thinning each tile to 15–30k points matters far less than tile size. Its walls are thin planes, often slightly off the face or drawn across open space.

0.54

Cloud2BIM gets the corridors, not the rooms

Long corridor walls are right; the central light-well block, most partitions and every opening are missing. It needed three small changes to run on a handheld scan (below).

0.46 / 0.61

A video gets the layout, not the precision

From 3½ minutes of video, LingBot-Map rebuilt 194 m of corridor at the right scale (camera 1.6 m above the floor). Half of its wall surface lies within 20 cm of the LiDAR; the error stays at 3–15 cm along straight corridors and jumps to 40–95 cm at the stairwell and one turn. The best video wall model (our block method) reaches F 0.46 at 10 cm and 0.61 at 25 cm, against 0.94 / 0.95 for the LiDAR model in the same area.

0–2 / 8

Closed doors defeat everyone

Of the 8 recessed office doors on the Room 319 wall, SceneScript finds at most 2; Cloud2BIM and our openings detector find none. The route guide places Room 319 by counting the regular 2.75 m door spacing instead.

07 · Set-up notesWhat it took to run them

MethodChange or fixWhy
Cloud2BIMSlab-density threshold 0.6 → 0.35It finds floors and ceilings as height levels holding ≥ 60 % of the densest level's points. A handheld scanner sees the ceiling at ~40 % of the floor's density, so the ceiling (and so the storey) was missed.
Cloud2BIMLaTeX off in its plots; room-zone step made non-fatalNo LaTeX on the server; its IfcSpace zone segmentation crashed on this storey (walls and slabs are unaffected). 7 of its 101 IFC walls do not triangulate.
SceneScripttorchsparse from its master branch (2.1)The "v2.1.0" branch actually builds 2.0, which puts the batch index last: the encoder then saw 481 scenes instead of one and decoded NaN.
SceneScriptPyTorch 2.4 (CUDA 12.1) restored; GCC 12 in the envpip replaced PyTorch while installing torchsparse's dependencies; CUDA 12.1 rejects the system's GCC 13.
LingBot-MapFlashInfer 0.6.17 with its prebuilt kernels; GPU TSDF fusion (Open3D VoxelBlockGrid)FlashInfer 0.7 compiles its kernels on first use and needs build tools the server lacks. Open3D's older TSDF volume returned empty meshes on these depth maps.
LingBot-MapWindowed inference, long-sequence checkpoint, keyframe every 2nd frame2,582 frames exceed the 320-frame range it was trained on; windows of 128 keyframes are aligned to each other by the model.
LingBot-MapDepth filtering before fusion (confidence top half, sky, depth jumps); surfaces kept only if 3 frames agreeEach frame's depth disagrees with its neighbours by about 2.5 %, which leaves floating layers if the depth maps are simply stacked.
SceneScriptTiling; failed or empty tiles skipped and countedThe floor is far larger than its 32 m input cube. A few tiles return nothing or crash in the decoder (2 of 131 at 8 m).
